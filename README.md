# The Flood Archive

**A source-led flood investigation game for macOS, iPadOS, and iPhone.** The English-language experience uses an adventurous, mysterious field-dossier style. Players assemble geological, fossil, archaeological, and textual clues into their own theory about Noah’s Flood. Each case file identifies its location and links the observation to its source, while labeling flood-geology interpretations as interpretations.

## Languages

The interface and investigation content launch in English. Use the English / Română segmented control in the sidebar to switch to Romanian; the choice is saved on the device. Original publication titles remain in their published language so sources are easy to find.

The game is an investigation interface, not a report of a newly discovered archive. Its named places, specimens, and ancient texts are real and linked to their sources; players assemble them into a theory.

## Open in Xcode

1. Copy this folder to your MacBook.
2. Open `TheFloodArchive.xcodeproj` in Xcode.
3. Select **My Mac** to run on macOS, or connect and select your iPad or iPhone.
4. For your own iPad or iPhone, sign in to Xcode with your Apple Account and select your **Personal Team** under **Signing & Capabilities**. On the device, enable Developer Mode if Xcode asks.
5. Press **Run**. Personal on-device development does not require the paid Apple Developer Program. App Store distribution is separate.

## Included

- One universal Xcode project for Mac, iPad, and iPhone, with a custom illustrated title scene, expedition map, clue collection, evidence wall, case theory, and reference-linked **Claim Audit**.
- Fourteen evidence files covering regional megafloods, the Black Sea rapid-inflow hypothesis, post-Ice-Age sea-level rise, marine fossils at elevation, Tapeats/Coconino megasequences, polystrate trees, dinosaur soft-tissue reports, unconformities, folded strata, catastrophic plate tectonics, RATE helium-in-zircon claims, fossil succession, and a fossil preserving a marine reptile giving birth.
- North American cases for the Channeled Scablands, Yukon mammoths, and Ashfall Fossil Beds in Nebraska.

- A timeline that keeps separate places, specimens, and geological periods distinct.

## Source labels

Each Claim Audit reference is labeled by source type, such as peer-reviewed research, government or museum record, historical expedition account, Bible text, or creationist-perspective research. The linked articles document the underlying observation or the pro-flood interpretation named on the card. The game presents a flood-forward investigation, but the player forms the final theory; it does not state that scientific research has proven one global flood.

Notable scientific and institutional records include the USGS review of the Missoula floods; peer-reviewed Black Sea research by Ryan and colleagues; the Barbados sea-level record; Grand Canyon and Yukon park/government geology; the Nebraska State Museum's Ashfall research; Schweitzer and colleagues' dinosaur-protein studies; sedimentology at Joggins; and the PLOS ONE report of the Chaohusaurus birth-position fossil.

The grass-filled Berezovka mammoth was found in Siberia, not North America. The Chaohusaurus birth-position fossil came from China. Nun cho ga is the separate mummified calf discovered in Yukon. The app distinguishes these well-known examples instead of relocating them to North America.

Images and video links require an internet connection. Third-party media remains on its publisher's website and is not bundled into this project. The hero illustration and map are original SwiftUI artwork; the map is labeled schematic.
